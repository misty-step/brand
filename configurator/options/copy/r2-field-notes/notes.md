# r2-field-notes

Field notes from a studio that builds. Round 2 copy for mistystep.io (studio) and stack.mistystep.io (stack).

## Voice
We write like a studio keeping notes as it builds: first person plural, warm and observational, exact about what we made and what our method says. Long sentences carry the detail, and a short one lands it.

## Headline candidates

### Studio
1. **Two weeks to see what's worth building.** (hero) The spell's "only step where you can see", said in plain business words and true to the plan. The contraction, set with a typographic apostrophe, breaks as "TWO WEEKS TO SEE / WHAT'S WORTH BUILDING." at 1440, 390 and 320 (CopyEditor's probe).
2. **We'll tell you what not to build.** (terms title) True to "no build when the plan says not to", and surprising from a studio that sells builds.
3. **Poppycock needed rooms, so we built Parlor.** (work title) A real field note: Parlor's site says Poppycock is why Parlor exists.
4. **We only step where we can see.** (close) The spell itself, owned by the studio; the body gives the name's origin, so it closes what the hero opens.
5. **We build things, then write down how.** (footer) Studio and method in one breath; the stack site is the written-down half.
6. **From first call to first build.** (process title) The engagement as a route, with both ends named.
7. **Bring us one process.** A warm, direct opening; it now starts the hero sub.
8. **We ship something before week two ends.** The quick win as a date. Dropped: "something" is limp, and the deadline only restates "during the two weeks".
9. **We keep the first step small.** The brand's short step in the first person. Dropped as limp inventory.
10. **We start with one process and one quick win.** Plain and exact. Dropped as inventory.

### Stack
1. **Our agents read this first.** (hero) First person and true: "this" resolves to the core, printed as the first section below.
2. **The rules we give our own agents.** The best alternative hero: a plain first-person description of the repo.
3. **We wrote down how we work.** (opens the lines section) The field-notes premise, unglamorous.
4. **Every session starts here.** (core title) Points at the code block; true of an always-on core.
5. **Eight lines in every session.** A number in place of an adjective; the page derives it from facts.ts CORE.
6. **What loads, and when.** (method title) One always-on core, and the rest on a trigger.
7. **We replayed six tasks, once each.** (evidence title) First person, with the denominator and the biggest limit in the title.
8. **Read it. Then make it yours.** (close) Nods to the guide's last chapter, "Make it yours".
9. **Say how you'll know it worked.** The Oracle in plain words, to the engineer.
10. **Name the check. Then start.** The Oracle as an instruction; too close to CopyA's done line to use.
